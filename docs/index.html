Core Idea

A distant goal misleads the planner; a nearby recorded anchor guides it.

Frozen visual world models already solve short-range tasks reliably. They collapse over long horizons not because imagination fails, but because whole-image latent distance loses directional signal beyond a few moves and prioritizes robot arms over manipulated objects.

  • Zero retraining: Build one sparse graph over the world model's own training frames.
  • Near, continuous targets: Aim the unchanged CEM planner at an orange anchor node a few moves ahead along the shortest path, penalizing episode switches (λ = 3) when contact matters.
Overview

Abstract

Latent world models plan in a compact latent space by predicting the outcomes of actions. Their planners judge a plan by the latent distance between its predicted end state and the goal image. We show that this distance fails in two distinct ways. First, it loses directional signal a few steps from the goal, causing distant goals to fail. Second, it weighs the robot arm over the manipulated object, causing manipulation to fail at contact. We measure both failures directly on recorded data by asking whether a lower latent distance corresponds to true progress toward the goal. Across five environments, six visual representations (including raw pixels and V-JEPA 2), and nine Meta-World tasks, this ranking test cleanly separates the two failures, and the graph distance to a goal predicts where closed-loop planning breaks down. Both failures share a single remedy: replacing the distant goal with a nearby target that lies on a recorded trajectory. We realize this principle with Graph Anchors, a training-free planner for frozen latent world models. It builds a sparse graph once over the model's own training frames and aims each planning step at a node a few moves ahead along the shortest path to the goal, keeping paths on recorded transitions wherever contact matters. On goals up to 400 steps away, Graph Anchors raise LeWM's success from 13% to 85% on PointMaze-Medium, from 8% to 87% on PointMaze-Large, and from 5% to 71% on Push-T, where stronger search toward the goal fails and where prior training-free planners break down under missing step horizons, subsampling shortcuts, or fragmented contact.

Key Takeaway: Before running any closed-loop rollout, offline goal graph distance correlates with LeWM's collapse (Spearman ρ = −0.90) and predicts where Graph Anchors deliver large gains (+0.82) versus where short-range planning already suffices (Reacher).
Offline Diagnosis

Two Root Failures of Visual Goal Costs

Rather than guessing why long-horizon rollouts fail, we test the latent goal cost directly on recorded frames before running any planner.

Failure 1 · Range

Directional signal vanishes within six graph hops

We measure ρ(h), the probability that a neighbor closer to the goal on a reference route also has a lower latent distance. Across navigation tasks, ρ(h) begins near 0.91–1.00 right next to the goal and drops to near-chance (0.50–0.59) within six hops. Testing six visual representations on identical frames (raw pixels, DINOv2-S, CLIP, SigLIP, video-trained V-JEPA 2, and LeWM) reveals the exact same decay curve, showing short range is inherent to comparing two whole images across corridors and walls.

Failure 2 · Focus

Whole-image distance weighs the arm over the object

Across all nine Meta-World object tasks we test, shifting only the robot hand by 5 cm costs 2.6× to 14× more in whole-image latent distance than leaving only the manipulated object off target, solving 0 of 72 episodes under exact dynamics. Keeping the released goal cost untouched and simply aiming at a recorded demonstration frame 20 steps ahead solves 65 of 72 episodes immediately.

Training-Free Planner

How Graph Anchors Work

Keep the frozen world model and its CEM search untouched. Replace only the distant goal with a nearby recorded anchor node along the shortest route through training frames.

01 · SPARSE GRAPH

Full-dataset 1-block frame graph

Encode every 5th frame of the world model's own training data once. Add directed temporal edges (weight 1) along each episode and cross-episode k-NN bridges only within the fixed 1-block distance threshold τ, indexing 199k nodes in 72.9 MB with 0.00% sparse-data shortcuts.

02 · SHORTEST-PATH ANCHOR

Span K & contact switch cost λ

Attach the current observation and goal image, run cached Dijkstra, and select the node K moves ahead. Navigation uses K = 6, λ = 1. Where object contact matters, the contact setting (K = 2, λ = 3) penalizes episode switches, cutting Push-T switches 4.2× and keeping continuous pushes intact.

03 · PASS-THROUGH MPC

Closest-approach CEM execution

Score each imagined CEM rollout by its minimum latent distance to the active anchor along the horizon rather than only its final step, and execute up to that closest approach so the agent passes smoothly through anchors without stalling.
